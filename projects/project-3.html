Academic project Networking

Quality of Service (QoS) Simulation Lab

A NET 301 academic project exploring network traffic management, prioritization, congestion handling, and Quality of Service through research and physical simulation.

Overview

This project, titled Operation: Fiesta Night Rescue, focuses on understanding how Quality of Service (QoS) helps manage different types of network traffic. The activity examines a network scenario where multiple traffic classes share a limited WAN connection.

The report covers traffic analysis, bandwidth allocation, queue management, traffic marking, congestion avoidance, and the differences between traffic shaping and policing.

Problem

The simulated network scenario involves a 100 Mbps LAN connected to a 2.048 Mbps WAN link. The combined peak traffic demand reaches 2,836 kbps, exceeding the WAN capacity by 788 kbps. This creates a congestion problem that requires proper traffic prioritization and bandwidth management.

Proposed QoS Approach

The project examines the use of Low Latency Queuing (LLQ) for voice traffic and Class-Based Weighted Fair Queuing (CBWFQ) for other traffic classes. It also discusses DiffServ, DSCP marking, Weighted Random Early Detection (WRED), and traffic shaping and policing.

Key Activities

  • Traffic analysis: Identifying different network traffic types and their bandwidth requirements.
  • Queue management: Comparing FIFO, WFQ, CBWFQ, and LLQ.
  • Bandwidth allocation: Assigning bandwidth based on traffic priority.
  • Traffic marking: Studying Layer 2 CoS and Layer 3 DSCP markings.
  • Congestion avoidance: Understanding WRED and its behavior.
  • Shaping and policing: Comparing how network traffic is controlled.
  • Packet Jeepney simulation: Comparing FIFO and LLQ + CBWFQ using 20 physical packet slips across two rounds.

Simulation Results

During the physical simulation, the first round used FIFO, while the second round used LLQ + CBWFQ. The report showed that LLQ + CBWFQ protected all voice, video, and payment packets, while FIFO dropped one voice packet.

The simulation demonstrated how prioritizing important traffic can improve the handling of network packets during congestion, although lower-priority traffic may experience more drops.

My Contribution

I served as the Team Lead / Project Manager. I also personally narrated and presented the physical simulation, explaining the FIFO and QoS approaches during the two demonstration rounds.

What I Learned

This project strengthened my understanding of network traffic management, bandwidth allocation, queueing mechanisms, and congestion handling. It also improved my ability to explain networking concepts through practical activities and presentations.

Scope and Limitations

This is an academic networking project based on a simulated network scenario and a physical classroom demonstration. The Packet Jeepney activity is a simplified representation of packet handling and does not reproduce the full behavior of a production network.
